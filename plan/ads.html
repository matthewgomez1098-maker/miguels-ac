Customer acquisition plan

Getting more customers

Where the advertising money goes, in what order, and how to tell within 30 days whether it is working. Built around one fact about this trade: almost nobody shops for an HVAC contractor until something breaks — so the job is to be the most findable, most credible option at the exact moment it does.

The strategy in one line

Own the free map results first. Buy the highest-intent clicks second. Use Meta for the off-season and for people who already know you.

Spending on ads before the free foundations are in place is the most common way small contractors waste money. A paid click that lands on a business with four reviews converts a fraction as well as the same click landing on one with sixty — you pay the same either way.

Phase 0

Do these before spending a dollar

None of this costs money. All of it raises the return on every dollar spent later, and the first two are the highest-value work on this entire page.

  1. Claim and finish the Google Business Profile

    Verify the business, set the primary category to HVAC contractor, add service areas for every city, upload real job photos, and set hours.

    Why first: the map pack sits above every paid result. For "ac repair near me" it is the majority of clicks, and it costs nothing. No ad campaign outperforms owning this.

  2. Get to 25+ real reviews, then keep going

    Text every finished job a direct review link the same day, while the house is cold and the customer is happy. Aim for a steady trickle, not a burst.

    Why: review count and recency are among the strongest local ranking factors, and they lift conversion on every other channel at once. Do not buy reviews — the FTC treats fake reviews as illegal, and Google removes them.

  3. Put the real license number on everything

    The website and all four print pieces currently show a blank line where CSLB Lic. # goes.

    Why: California requires it on contractor advertising (B&P Code §7030.5). Google Local Services Ads will not approve the account without a verified license either — so this blocks the single best paid channel.

  4. Set up call tracking before the first ad runs

    A tracking number that forwards to Miguel's phone, plus Google Analytics and the Meta pixel on the site.

    Why: this trade converts on the phone, not on forms. Without call tracking you cannot tell which channel produced a booking, and every budget decision after month one becomes guesswork.

  5. Buy the domain

    The site is live at the GitHub Pages address. A real domain makes the ads, the business card and the profile all point somewhere that looks permanent.

    Why: Google Business Profile weighs a matching website, and a github.io URL on a business card reads as temporary to a homeowner.

  6. Done — the website and the print set

    43 pages with a city page for each of the 29 service areas, plus door hanger, business card, flyer and Instagram artwork.

    The city pages are what let search ads send someone in Fontana to a Fontana page instead of a generic homepage. That lifts Quality Score, which lowers what you pay per click.

When to spend

Seasonality decides the budget, not the calendar

HVAC demand in the Inland Empire is violently seasonal. Spending evenly across twelve months means overpaying in February and running out of budget in the week that matters most. Both series below are shares of an annual total, so they sit on one scale — the gap between them is the whole point.

Google

How to structure the Google side

Three products, in the order they should be switched on. Do not start all three at once — you will not be able to tell which one worked.

1. Local Services Ads

Pay per lead · Google Guaranteed badge

Sits above everything, including search ads. You pay per lead rather than per click, and you can dispute leads that were not real. Requires license and insurance verification plus a background check.

Start here. Highest intent, and the badge does credibility work that a new business otherwise has to buy with reviews. Blocked until the license number is real.

2. Search ads

Pay per click · highest control

Catches the "my AC just died" searches LSA misses. Expensive in summer, so it lives or dies on tight targeting and negative keywords.

Add in month two, once LSA has produced a baseline cost per booked job to compare against.

3. Performance Max

Automated · lowest control

Google spreads budget across YouTube, Display, Gmail and Maps automatically.

Not yet. It needs conversion data to aim with. Run it before you have that and it spends the budget learning what the other two already know.

Search campaign skeleton

  • Campaign splitOne per region, so budget can follow the heat: Inland EmpireSan Gabriel Valley LA County
  • Ad groupsOne per job type, never mixed — repair, installation, heat pump, mini-split, maintenance. Each points at its matching service page.
  • Landing pagesCity page for geo terms, service page for job terms. Never the homepage — relevance is a Quality Score input, and Quality Score is a direct discount on cost per click.
  • Keywordsac repair near me · emergency ac repair [city] · ac not blowing cold · hvac contractor [city] · ac replacement quote
  • Negativesjobs hiring salary diy how to parts free training window unit portable — add to a shared list and review weekly for the first month
  • TargetingRadius around each service area, set to presence — people in the area, not people merely interested in it. That one setting stops budget leaking to other states.
  • ScheduleBid up during hours someone actually answers. An emergency click at 11pm that reaches voicemail is money burned.
  • ExtensionsCall, location, and sitelinks to the service pages. On mobile, call-only ads for emergency terms — the goal is a ringing phone.
  • BiddingStart on Maximise Clicks with a cap to gather data, then move to Maximise Conversions once call tracking has fed in ~30 conversions.

Meta

How to structure Facebook and Instagram

Nobody scrolling Instagram is searching for an HVAC contractor. Meta is not for catching demand — it is for building recognition before the breakdown, and for staying in front of people who already visited the site.

Retargeting

Smallest budget, best return

Anyone who visited the site or engaged with the page in the last 30 days. They already know the name.

Creative: the "Broken A/C? Call us today" story, plus a real before/after from a job.

Local awareness

Homeowners, tight radius

Radius around the cities being worked that week. Homeowner-leaning targeting, ages 30–65.

Creative: short vertical video of an actual repair. Face, truck, hands, a fixed unit. This out-performs a designed graphic almost every time.

Maintenance plan

Spring and autumn only

Lead form for tune-ups before the season turns. The cheapest customer to acquire is one who is already on a plan.

Creative: the "Repair, install, maintain" post, pointed at the maintenance plans page.

Post organically before paying to boost anything. A page with three posts and no reviews converts badly no matter what it costs to get someone there. Two posts a week — finished jobs, the crew, a genuinely useful tip about filters or thermostats — for a month before the first paid dollar.

Beyond the big two

The rest, honestly assessed

Worth doing

  • NextdoorNeighbourhood recommendations carry unusual weight for trades. Free to claim, cheap to advertise on, and a single recommendation thread can outperform a month of ads.
  • Referral offerAn existing customer telling a neighbour is the cheapest lead you will ever get. A standing credit for a referral that books costs a fraction of a paid lead.
  • Truck and yard signageA wrapped truck parked at a job all day is advertising to the exact street being worked. Cheapest cost per impression available to a local trade.
  • Door hangersAlready built. Highest return on the streets either side of a job just completed — same houses, same era, same equipment.

Approach with caution

  • Angi / ThumbtackLeads are resold to three or four contractors at once, so you are bidding on speed and price against people who already have the same phone number. Useful to fill a slow week, corrosive as a main channel.
  • Yelp AdsReal volume for HVAC, but the contracts are hard to exit and spend is difficult to control. If tried, set a hard monthly cap.
  • Boosting postsThe Boost button is not the same as a campaign. It optimises for engagement, which buys likes rather than calls. Use Ads Manager with a Leads or Calls objective.
  • Buying reviewsIllegal under FTC rules, and Google removes them. It also puts the Google Business Profile at risk — the one asset here that cannot be replaced with money.

Reality check

What this page is and is not

Every cost figure here is an industry benchmark for HVAC in Southern California, not a measurement of Miguel's business. They exist to make the plan concrete and to give the calculator sensible defaults — nothing more.

After 30 days of tracked spend, replace every one of them with what actually happened. At that point this becomes a real forecast instead of a sensible starting hypothesis. Until the license number and call tracking are in place, none of the paid channels should be switched on at all.
